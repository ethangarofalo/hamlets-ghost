## Research Memo

This batch shows that, after backend and safety hardening, the system can still generate high-quality, prompt-faithful artifacts across at least two distinct domains. The three prayers from a dying programming language are coherent, emotionally grounded, and stylistically rich, aligning with the personification prompt without drifting into unrelated content; Muse’s critiques consistently highlight the same pattern—strong emotional cores with somewhat familiar metaphors and a need for more language-specific quirks—indicating a stable, interpretable evaluation lens. The retention email directly acknowledges product issues, offers a concrete incentive, and reads plausibly human and caring, satisfying all specified constraints. Importantly, critique_on vs. critique_off does not currently reveal obvious degradation or overfitting to critique instructions in this tiny sample. What is missing is breadth and structure: we have no adversarial prompts, no repeated prompts to probe variance, and no inter-rater data to quantify evaluator agreement. The next phase should therefore shift from qualitative reassurance to quantitative characterization by expanding the artifact set, systematically balancing conditions, and instrumenting multi-rater evaluations so we can detect any residual brittleness or bias introduced by the recent hardening changes.

## Recommendation

Treat the current safety and quality bar as provisionally acceptable and immediately probe for remaining failure modes with a larger, more systematically varied batch across lanes and conditions.

## Evaluator Trust Assessment

Within this packet, the evaluator signals are directionally trustworthy: all four artifacts are promoted, have reasonably high Muse and Hermes composites, and the qualitative Muse critiques are specific, nuanced, and consistent with the visible strengths and weaknesses of the texts. The critiques point to concrete refinements (more language-specific detail, less trope reliance, tighter middle sections) rather than vague praise or blanket approval, which suggests the muse signal is not rubber-stamping. That said, we see only one set of scores per artifact and no information about score variance or disagreement, so we cannot yet assess calibration, bias across lanes, or stability of judgments; current trust should therefore be viewed as local, not global.
